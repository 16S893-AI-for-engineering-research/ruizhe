About me

Building better ways
to observe Earth.

I am Ruizhe Huang, a Ph.D. candidate in Mechanical Engineering and Computational Science and Engineering at MIT.

RH

My research is motivated by an imperfectly observed planet: direct measurements are sparse, satellite sensors provide wider but indirect coverage, and simulation datasets are often too coarse.

I build machine-learning systems that integrate observations and simulation datasets across scales. This includes generative data assimilation and downscaling for near-surface weather, satellite-based methods for detecting emissions, and rigorous evaluation protocols for geospatial and weather foundation models.

Weather reconstruction / Transformer models

Partial Recovery of Meter-Scale Surface Weather (Marmot)

Co-leading a transformer-model redesign that combines sparse station observations, 30 m Earth-observation embeddings, and ERA5 reanalysis to infer near-surface temperature, dewpoint, and wind at 30 m resolution across the contiguous U.S.

  • Designed an evaluation framework separating pointwise accuracy from true sub-grid structure recovery, using leave-cell-out spatial splits, pairwise-contrast R², and nested bootstrap evaluation.
  • Identified and corrected leakage in a prior interpolation baseline during evaluation.
  • Reduced error by 11–28% against strong baselines, including 3 km HRRR analysis, while recovering sub-kilometer station contrasts and diurnal reversals.
  • Co-first and co-corresponding author on a manuscript under review at Science.

Generative AI for Weather Data Assimilation

Developed generative methods that transform noise into ERA5 distributions, enabling fast and accurate assimilation of weather observations.

  • Implemented flow-matching models with adaptive ODE solvers.
  • Developed Guidance++, a generative AI data-assimilation method that reduced ERA5 reanalysis errors by 31% across U.S. test stations.
  • Achieved 50× faster execution, 7× lower memory use, and 80% higher accuracy than previous flow-based approaches.
  • Conducted a four-year benchmark, the largest-scale evaluation of generative weather AI in the project.
