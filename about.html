About me

Building better ways
to observe Earth.

I am Ruizhe Huang, a Ph.D. student in Mechanical Engineering and Computational Science and Engineering at MIT.

RH

My research is motivated by an imperfectly observed planet: weather stations are sparse, satellite sensors have limits, and critical environmental signals often exist below the resolution of our best datasets.

I build machine-learning systems that integrate observations across scales. This includes generative data assimilation and probabilistic downscaling for near-surface weather, satellite-based methods for detecting emissions, and rigorous evaluation protocols for geospatial and weather foundation models.

Before beginning my Ph.D., I completed an M.S. in Mechanical Engineering at MIT and a B.S. in Theoretical and Applied Mechanics at Peking University.

Research interests

01

Recovering unresolved Earth-system variability

Fusing sparse in-situ observations with Earth observations to infer what existing measurements leave unresolved.

02

Generative data assimilation & downscaling

Building probabilistic models for high-resolution, near-surface weather reconstruction.

03

Remote sensing of emissions

Using satellite data to detect and quantify greenhouse-gas and pollutant emissions.

04

Evaluation for geospatial foundation models

Designing benchmarks that distinguish genuine spatial recovery from interpolation or leakage.
