Good environmental models should do more than produce a low average error. They should recover meaningful structure at the scales where people, ecosystems, and infrastructure experience weather.

01Evaluate what matters.

01 / Evaluation

Meter-scale weather recovery

Point accuracy is not the same as spatial structure.

For Marmot, I designed evaluation procedures that test whether a model recovers variation between nearby stations, not simply whether it predicts each location well on average. Leave-cell-out splits, pairwise contrasts, and nested bootstrapping make that distinction measurable.

02 / Modeling

Generative assimilation

A distribution is more useful than a single guess.

Generative weather models make it possible to represent uncertainty while assimilating observations. Flow matching and adaptive ODE solvers provided a practical route from noise to ERA5 distributions in my work on Guidance++.

03 / Observation

Satellite NO₂ plumes

Detectability is a physical and statistical question.

Whether a plume is visible from space depends on atmosphere, environment, and sensor conditions. Mapping this variability across more than 6,000 power plants helps make downstream emissions analysis more interpretable.
