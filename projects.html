Can we trust a power plant’s satellite-estimated emissions?

Testing the limits of TROPOMI estimates of power-plant NOx—from single overpasses to annual totals and a global pilot.

01

WHY THIS MATTERS

Most power plants are not measured at the stack.

U.S. plants report hourly emissions through continuous emissions monitoring systems. Most plants elsewhere do not, so inventories rely on fuel statistics and assumed emission factors.

TROPOMI can see the NO2 plume drifting from a large plant. Our neural network turns that plume—along with wind, cloud, viewing geometry, and surface data—into an hourly NOx estimate.

This project asks where those estimates are reliable, where they fail, and what evidence is needed before reporting an annual number.

02

RESEARCH PLAN

01

How many overpasses are enough?

Separate random overpass noise from plant-specific bias, calibrate uncertainty at the plant level, and derive an observation count for a target precision such as ±30%.

LIMITS
02

Which plants work best—and why?

Use five-fold plant-level cross-validation to expand the held-out cohort from 7 to 46 plants, then test plant, scene, and sampling drivers of error.

DRIVERS
03

Can sparse scenes yield annual totals?

Distinguish model error from sampling bias caused by clear-sky retrievals, afternoon overpasses, and intermittent plant operation.

ANNUAL
04

Does the method transfer globally?

Validate on the United States in 2018, then pilot selected plants abroad while flagging cases outside the model’s training support.

GLOBAL
03

KEY HYPOTHESIS

More data cannot average away systematic error.

For N overpasses, plant-level error has a persistent bias term and a random term that shrinks with averaging:

MSE(N) = σb2 + σε2 / N

Current results imply random error of about 790 lb h−1 and a systematic floor near 540 lb h−1. If that holds, calibration matters more than collecting many additional scenes.

04

ANNUAL ESTIMATES

Separate prediction error from when the satellite happens to look.

TOTAL ERRORP − A
=
MODEL ERRORP − S
+
SAMPLING ERRORS − A

P is the model mean over observed hours, S is CEMS over those same hours, and A is the true annual CEMS mean. This decomposition makes clear whether the model is wrong or the satellite simply observed an unrepresentative slice of the year.
